The BABS agenda

A Five-Point Agenda for a Stronger Ibarapa East

Listen. Think. Advocate. Answer.

These priorities set out where Hon. Ogunlade intends to focus his advocacy, his questions and his time if elected to the Oyo State House of Assembly. They are offered as a public standard for residents to examine and hold him to.

01 · PRIORITY ONE

Farming & Food Security

Support the people who feed Ibarapa East.

Why it matters

Agriculture remains a major source of livelihood across Eruwa, Lanlate and neighbouring communities. Farming households do better when they can reach inputs, financing, storage, transport and dependable markets, not only land and labour. Farmers are contributors to local economic growth as well as producers of food.

Proposed priorities

  • Advocate for farmer support and better access to inputs, information and appropriate modern farming practices.
  • Promote better market access for local produce.
  • Encourage practical agricultural partnerships connecting producers with relevant support programmes.
  • Support farmer cooperatives as a base for shared strength and bargaining power.
  • Give attention to post-harvest management, small-scale agro-processing and rural roads to reduce avoidable losses.
  • Open opportunities for young people to participate in agricultural value chains.

The role of a legislator

Through budget scrutiny, policy discussion, constituency consultation and engagement with the ministries and agencies responsible for agriculture and rural development, a legislator can keep farmers’ needs on the agenda where funding and policy are decided.

How progress will be reported

Farmers’ concerns raised, agencies engaged and responses received will be shared publicly at town halls.

02 · PRIORITY TWO

Opportunity & Jobs

Open doors for young people and small businesses.

Why it matters

Many young people face unemployment, limited access to training and difficulty starting out. Traders, artisans, transport operators and small-business owners can also struggle with financing, high costs and access to markets. A fair economy should open doors for both.

Proposed priorities

  • Promote vocational, technical and skills pathways that match identifiable market needs.
  • Encourage entrepreneurship, mentoring and practical business guidance for young people.
  • Support small-business and enterprise development, including traders, artisans and farmers.
  • Advocate for job-creating partnerships and transparent access to government programmes.
  • Encourage digital literacy so young people can explore online enterprise and remote work.
  • Move beyond temporary, election-season relief towards lasting livelihoods.

The role of a legislator

A legislator cannot create a jobs system alone or guarantee business success. The office can advocate sound policies, scrutinise public programmes, support responsible budget priorities and draw attention to barriers facing local enterprise.

How progress will be reported

Opportunities identified, programmes engaged and practical information on how residents can access them.

03 · PRIORITY THREE

Community Infrastructure

Basic facilities that communities rely on every day.

Why it matters

Infrastructure matters for what it makes possible. A road connects farmers to markets, children to school and families to care. Reliable electricity supports small enterprise. Safe water protects public health. Maintenance matters as much as new construction because neglected assets waste public money.

Proposed priorities

  • Promote attention to local roads and mobility across Eruwa, Lanlate and other communities.
  • Advocate for maintained public facilities, giving maintenance proper weight alongside new projects.
  • Engage communities on local project needs and record and rank pressing needs for roads, electricity and water.
  • Engage relevant ministries and agencies and advocate for appropriate budget attention.
  • Monitor public commitments within the limits of legislative authority.
  • Support efforts to expand access to safe water in communities.

The role of a legislator

A legislator can document needs, raise them in budget and policy discussions, question responsible officials through proper procedures and follow public commitments through to results.

How progress will be reported

Community needs listed, how priorities were set and each project’s status: proposed, approved, under implementation or completed.

04 · PRIORITY FOUR

Uplifting Education & Health

Stronger learning environments and primary healthcare.

Why it matters

Education secures the constituency’s future, and healthcare is one of the clearest tests of whether government responds to ordinary people. For students, classroom conditions matter. For families in emergencies, the condition of nearby health facilities affects the speed, cost and quality of care.

Proposed priorities

  • Support learning environments for young people, including improved public school infrastructure and teacher development.
  • Explore scholarship and bursary opportunities through relevant institutions and help students from less privileged backgrounds stay in school.
  • Give vocational and technical education equal attention alongside academic pathways.
  • Advocate for stronger primary healthcare: essential equipment, qualified personnel, medicines and reliable referral arrangements.
  • Support medical outreach and promote health awareness and responsive community services.
  • Pay particular attention to vulnerable residents, including older people, widows, persons with disabilities and families facing hardship.

The role of a legislator

A legislator does not run schools or hospitals, but can raise documented concerns, scrutinise education and health spending within the Assembly’s remit, engage responsible authorities and press for a fairer spread of services. A scholarship can help one student, but a lasting agenda must also look at classrooms, learning materials and facilities.

How progress will be reported

Education and health concerns raised, responses received and measurable objectives set for each commitment.

05 · PRIORITY FIVE

Security & Social Cohesion

A safer, more united Ibarapa East.

Why it matters

Peace is the foundation of every other priority. A community that feels safe and respected can farm, trade, study and build. Traditional institutions, religious bodies and community associations help settle local concerns and strengthen social bonds.

Proposed priorities

  • Encourage community-level dialogue and cooperation across wards.
  • Support peaceful, respectful engagement across wards, parties and faiths.
  • Advocate for responsive public safety coordination between communities and responsible agencies.
  • Pursue balanced development across the constituency, with attention to communities that might otherwise be overlooked.
  • Weigh competing priorities fairly, using transparent criteria, and explain the reasons for decisions.
  • Serve every constituent, including those who did not vote for the winner.

The role of a legislator

A legislator can convene conversations, carry community concerns to the right authorities and set a standard of civility in public life. Equity does not mean identical resources for every ward. It means honest assessment of need and willingness to explain.

How progress will be reported

Concerns raised from each ward and how they were taken forward.

Public accountability

Our Accountability Standard

Across all five priorities, Hon. Ogunlade states that he will apply a simple discipline of public reporting.

Regular town halls

Hold regular meetings where residents can raise concerns, ask questions and receive explanations.

Ward-level engagement

Maintain ongoing community engagement in every ward.

Public progress updates

Monitor development priorities and report on them publicly.

Clear institutional roles

Explain which issues fall within the executive arm of government or other institutions.

Updates will distinguish four stages: proposed, approved, under implementation and completed. Accountability means explaining what has been done, what remains, what constraints exist and what comes next.

The constitutional role

What One Legislator Can Deliver

The Oyo State House of Assembly considers bills, examines public finances through the legislative process, contributes to oversight and represents constituency concerns. Within that role, Hon. Ogunlade says he will advocate for the constituency’s priorities, sponsor or support relevant legislation, scrutinise public programmes, question responsible officials through proper procedures, bring local concerns into policy and budget discussions, and keep structured channels of communication open with constituents.
